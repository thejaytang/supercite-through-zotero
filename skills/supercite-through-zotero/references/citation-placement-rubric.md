# Citation Placement Rubric

Use this rubric for in-text citation placement, citation dumping, and multi-source synthesis.

## Main Distinction

Multiple references are acceptable when they support the same narrow claim. They are risky when they are attached to several different claims, a whole paragraph, or a vague literature summary without clear source roles.

## Acceptable Multi-Source Synthesis

Risk `0-1` when:

- The sentence makes one precise claim.
- The cited works plausibly belong to the same evidence role, such as theory, method, empirical finding, review, dataset, or benchmark.
- The cluster is close to the claim it supports.
- The paragraph explains how sources relate, rather than listing them as decoration.

Typical recommendation: keep the cluster, or group sources by role only if clarity improves.

## Suspicious Citation Dumping

Risk `2` when:

- A sentence contains multiple claims but all citations appear at the end.
- A paragraph develops several claims and places all citations in the final sentence.
- Five or more references are attached to a broad claim such as "many studies show" without grouping source roles.
- Different source types are mixed without explanation, such as theory, method papers, empirical papers, and reviews in one cluster.

Typical recommendation: split the sentence, move citations closer to supported clauses, or introduce source groups.

## High-Risk Citation Problems

Risk `3` when:

- A claim depends on a citation that is missing from the reference list.
- The text attributes a specific result, sample, dataset, page number, or method to a source that cannot be verified from provided material.
- The same citation appears to be used to support unrelated claims in different places.
- A citation cluster appears after a paragraph that contains claims from several fields or time periods.

Typical recommendation: mark `needs source check` or request the source abstract/full text before revision.

## Review Questions

For each claim unit:

1. What exact claim needs support?
2. Is the citation close to that claim?
3. Are all citations in the cluster serving the same role?
4. Would moving or splitting citations change meaning?
5. Is source content required before judging support?

## Safe Revision Patterns

- Move a citation after the clause it supports.
- Split a mixed sentence into one claim per sentence.
- Replace a generic pile with grouped synthesis: reviews, experiments, longitudinal studies, qualitative studies, theoretical sources, or methods.
- Use narrative attribution for a source with a specific finding.
- Keep the existing cluster when all sources support one narrow claim.
